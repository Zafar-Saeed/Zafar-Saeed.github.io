## Abstract

Scholars need to keep up with an exponentially increasing flood of scientific papers. As a result, users' reading behaviour is rapidly evolving, making it crucial for online scientific documents to be modelled in a well-structured layout to support their reading behaviour. Scientific documents are well-structured, but researchers have identified problems in online reading due to the poor layout and complex structure. This paper addresses issues like navigation, overview, customisation of structural units, and accessibility of cross-references with minimal effort. Three layouts, Spatial Section Zooming Layout (SSZL), Tabbed Section Layout (TSL), and Collapsing Section Layout (CSL), are designed. Each layout supports different levels of customisation over the structural units of the scientific document, allowing readers to adjust and personalise the layout according to their reading needs with seamless interactivity. The usability assessment compares the newly designed layouts with two widely used baseline layouts: Linear Layout (LL) and Overview+Detail Layout (ODL). The evaluation primarily employs five usability metrics, suggesting that SSZL is a more usable layout for online reading to support skimming, non-linear, and linear reading behaviours. Meanwhile, results from the task-based evaluation show that CSL is the most effective, followed by SSZL, in enhancing reading proficiency compared to the baseline layouts.
